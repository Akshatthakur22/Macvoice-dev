Voice typing is easy alone in a room. With colleagues around, or family at home, it takes a bit of thought.

## Hardware matters most

- **Headset or AirPods:** the mic is close to your mouth, so you can speak quietly and it mostly ignores other voices.
- **Avoid the laptop mic** in shared spaces: it hears the whole room.
- **Select the right mic** in your settings so it doesn't fall back to the built-in one ([microphone guide](/blog/best-microphone-for-dictation)).

## Speak quietly, not in a whisper

A **low conversational voice** works well with a close mic. Whispering changes how words sound and usually hurts accuracy.

## Choose push-to-talk, not always-listening

Tools you **start and stop with a shortcut** (Apple Dictation, [MacVoice](/)) only listen while you choose. Always-listening tools like Voice Control may pick up conversations around you. Voice Control has "go to sleep" for that.

## Etiquette

- **Let people know** you dictate; it stops them thinking you're talking to them.
- **Don't dictate in quiet zones,** libraries or meetings; switch to the keyboard.
- **Avoid long sessions** at peak times; batch your dictation for quieter periods or a booth.

## Confidentiality

Anyone nearby hears what you dictate. Keep **client names, health details and personal data** for private rooms, or use initials and fill in details by keyboard ([confidential dictation](/blog/dictating-confidential-information)).

## Background noise

Coffee machines and chatter cause errors. Besides a close mic:

- Turn your face **away from the noise**.
- Pause while a loud noise passes, then continue.
- Check names and numbers before sending.

## Video calls and dictation

Don't dictate during calls: your call app and your dictation tool will compete for the microphone, and colleagues will hear everything. Finish the call, then dictate your follow-up notes. If you're in back-to-back meetings, a quick ten-second voice capture between calls ([notes workflow](/blog/dictate-in-notion-and-obsidian)) is a good compromise.

## Booths and quiet rooms

Many offices have phone booths. They're ideal for longer dictation sessions: a document draft, a long report, a batch of emails. Book 20–30 minutes, dictate everything, then go back to your desk to edit by keyboard.

## At home

Same rules: a headset helps you speak quietly near sleeping partners or kids. If you dictate in the evening, a quick read-through matters more because tired speech tends to be less clear.
