Your Mac can type what you say in almost any app, and it's built in. This guide walks through turning it on, using it well, and the few settings worth changing.

![Where to find Dictation settings on a Mac](/blog/diagrams/mac-dictation-settings.svg "Dictation lives in System Settings → Keyboard.")

## Turn Dictation on

1. Open the **Apple menu → System Settings**.
2. Click **Keyboard** in the sidebar.
3. Scroll down to **Dictation** and switch it on, then click **Enable**.

macOS may ask whether you want to share audio recordings with Apple to improve Dictation. That's optional; you can say no and Dictation still works.

The first time, your Mac may download the speech files for your language. That takes a minute or two on a normal connection.

## Start and stop dictating

Click into any text box (a Notes page, an email, a Google Doc), then start Dictation with any of these:

- Press the **Microphone key** (F5 on recent Mac keyboards). A short press starts Dictation; holding it starts Siri instead.
- Use your **Dictation shortcut** (you can change it, see below).
- Choose **Edit → Start Dictation** from the menu bar.

A small microphone appears next to your cursor while it's listening. To stop, press **Escape**, the Microphone key, or the shortcut again. Dictation also stops by itself after about 30 seconds of silence.

## Add punctuation and line breaks

In supported languages, Dictation adds commas, periods and question marks automatically as you speak. You can also say them:

| Say | You get |
|---|---|
| comma | , |
| period / full stop | . |
| question mark | ? |
| exclamation mark | ! |
| new line | a line break |
| new paragraph | two line breaks |

Apple has a long list of other commands for symbols, capitals and spacing. See our [full Apple Dictation commands list](/blog/apple-dictation-commands).

## Fix mistakes as you go

- Words Dictation wasn't sure about are **underlined in blue**. Click one to choose an alternative.
- On an Apple silicon Mac you can just **type a correction while Dictation keeps listening**.
- Select a word and dictate again to replace it.

## Settings worth changing

All of these are under **System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation**:

- **Languages:** click *Edit* to add more. With several languages, click the language label next to the cursor (or press the Globe key) to switch. More in [dictating in more than one language](/blog/dictate-in-multiple-languages-mac).
- **Microphone source:** pick a specific microphone if Dictation keeps listening to the wrong one (like a webcam).
- **Shortcut:** choose a different key combination. See [how to change the dictation shortcut](/blog/change-dictation-shortcut-mac).
- **Auto-punctuation:** switch it off if you'd rather say every comma yourself. See [auto-punctuation explained](/blog/mac-dictation-auto-punctuation).

The settings page also tells you whether your dictation is **processed on your device** or sent to Apple. More on that in [is Mac dictation private?](/blog/is-mac-dictation-private)

## Tips for better results

- **Speak in full phrases**, not word by word. Speech engines use the surrounding words to work out what you meant.
- **Use a decent microphone** close to your mouth. AirPods and headsets usually beat a laptop mic in a noisy room ([microphone guide](/blog/best-microphone-for-dictation)).
- **Read it back** before sending. Names and jargon are where most errors happen.
