Lawyers, clinicians, consultants, HR teams and finance staff were among the earliest dictation users. The tools have changed a lot since dedicated dictation machines, and so have the risks.

> **Note:** This is practical, general guidance, not legal, medical or compliance advice. Your organisation's policies and your regulatory obligations come first.

## The two risks

1. **Digital:** where your audio and text are processed and stored.
2. **Physical:** who can hear you while you speak.

## Reducing digital risk

### Prefer on-device recognition

If speech is processed on your computer, there's no audio upload to secure, retain or disclose. See [on-device vs cloud](/blog/on-device-vs-cloud-speech-recognition).

- **Apple Dictation:** check System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation to confirm processing is on-device for your language.
- **Dictation apps:** the Wi-Fi-off test is the quickest check ([how](/blog/does-mac-dictation-work-offline)).

### Turn off "improve" options

Many tools ask to share recordings to improve their models. On a Mac: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → turn off *Improve Siri & Dictation*. Check equivalent settings in every app.

### Avoid unnecessary history

Some dictation apps keep a searchable history of everything you've said. Convenient, but it's another copy of confidential data. Prefer tools with no history, or turn it off.

### Watch the clipboard

Paste-based dictation puts your text on the system clipboard, where clipboard managers may store it indefinitely. Keyboard-input dictation avoids this ([details](/blog/dictation-without-clipboard-mac)).

### Remember the destination

On-device dictation protects your voice. The text still lives wherever you type it, so use approved document and email systems.

## Reducing physical risk

- Dictate sensitive material in a **private room**, not an open office or café ([open-office tips](/blog/dictating-in-an-open-office)).
- Use **initials or case numbers** instead of full names when speaking aloud, and fill in names afterwards.
- Be aware of **smart speakers** and other devices nearby.

## A checklist

- [ ] Tool processes speech on-device (verified with Wi-Fi off)
- [ ] Recording-sharing / training options off
- [ ] No dictation history kept (or disabled)
- [ ] Organisation has approved the tool
- [ ] Dictating in a private space
