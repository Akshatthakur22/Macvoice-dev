> **Important:** This article is general information, not medical advice. If you have ongoing pain, numbness or weakness in your hands, wrists or arms, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

If typing hurts, a voice keyboard is one of the most practical tools you can add. Here's how to use it well, and the mistakes to avoid.

## How dictation helps

Repetitive strain is about **load**: thousands of small repeated movements a day. Every paragraph you speak instead of type removes thousands of keystrokes. Occupational guidance often lists speech recognition alongside ergonomic changes, breaks and exercise as part of managing keyboard-related strain.

## What to dictate (and what not to)

| Dictate | Keep on the keyboard (or mouse) |
|---|---|
| Emails and messages | Short edits and corrections |
| Documents and reports | Shortcuts and navigation |
| Slack / Teams replies | Code syntax |
| AI prompts | Forms with many small fields |
| Notes and journals | |

Dictation won't help with **mouse use**. If pointing hurts, look at Voice Control ("show numbers", "click…"), or alternative pointing devices. See [Mac voice accessibility](/blog/voice-typing-accessibility-mac).

## Don't swap one strain for another

Heavy dictation uses your **voice muscles**. Clinicians have reported voice problems in people who switched to dictation all day without breaks. Protect your voice:

- Speak at **normal conversational volume**, not loudly.
- Use a **close microphone** so you don't need to project ([microphone guide](/blog/best-microphone-for-dictation)).
- **Drink water** and take short breaks every 20–30 minutes.
- **Alternate** voice and keyboard through the day; balance beats switching completely.

## Reduce corrections (they're keystrokes too)

Fixing errors by keyboard brings the strain back. Cut corrections by:

- Adding names and jargon as [custom words](/blog/add-custom-words-mac-dictation).
- Using a tool that removes "um"s and stutters automatically ([why](/blog/why-dictation-removes-filler-words)).
- Using voice to undo. In [MacVoice](/), "scratch that" deletes the last sentence without touching the keyboard.

## A low-strain setup on a Mac

1. One **global shortcut** you can press with one finger, or move to a foot pedal or programmable key.
2. **Polished mode** for prose (cleanup, "new line", "bullet point", "scratch that").
3. **Custom words** for your top 20 names and terms.
4. **Voice Control** for navigation if mouse use hurts.
5. **Breaks:** a timer every 25 minutes for hands *and* voice.
