Notes apps are where ideas go to live, and ideas arrive faster than you can type. Voice capture closes that gap.

## What's built in

| App | Built-in dictation | What to use |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Notes | Uses Apple Dictation | Apple Dictation or any dictation app |
| Notion (Mac) | No general dictation in the editor (AI Meeting Notes transcribes meetings) | Apple Dictation or a dictation app |
| Obsidian | None built in (community plugins exist) | Apple Dictation or a dictation app |

Dictation apps type into whatever is focused, so they work in all three, plus Bear, Craft, Logseq and anything else.

## Dictate structure, not just words

Notes are rarely paragraphs. Use voice commands for structure:

- **"new line"** for a line break
- **"new paragraph"** for a gap
- **"bullet point"** (in [MacVoice](/)) starts a `- ` list item, which Notion, Obsidian and Bear all turn into a bullet

Example: "Ideas for the launch bullet point short demo video bullet point post on Hacker News bullet point email existing users" becomes:

```
Ideas for the launch
- Short demo video
- Post on Hacker News
- Email existing users
```

## A capture workflow that sticks

1. **One inbox note.** Keep a single "Inbox" page pinned.
2. **Capture by voice, instantly.** Click in, press your shortcut, say the thought, stop. Ten seconds.
3. **Don't edit while capturing.** Typos and odd words are fine at this stage.
4. **Process later with the keyboard.** Once a day, move items to the right pages and clean them up.

Separating capture (voice) from organising (keyboard) is the same principle as [writing a first draft by voice](/blog/write-first-draft-by-voice).

## Tips

- **Headings:** type them; dictating "heading one" isn't supported by most tools.
- **Names and project codes:** add them as [custom words](/blog/add-custom-words-mac-dictation) so they're spelled right.
- **Privacy:** notes often hold personal thoughts. On-device dictation keeps your voice off third-party servers ([why it matters](/blog/is-mac-dictation-private)).
