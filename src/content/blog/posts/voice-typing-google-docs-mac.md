Google Docs has free voice typing built in, with a few strings attached. Here's how to use it on a Mac and what to do when it doesn't fit.

## Turn on Google Docs voice typing

1. Open your document in **Google Chrome**.
2. Choose **Tools → Voice typing**, or press **Cmd + Shift + S**.
3. Click the **microphone** and allow microphone access when Chrome asks.
4. Talk. Click the microphone again to stop.

You can choose the language from the menu above the microphone.

## What it can do

- **Punctuation by voice** ("comma", "period", "question mark", "new line", "new paragraph").
- **Editing and formatting commands** such as "select paragraph", "bold", "undo". Google says these work **only in English**, with your account and document language set to English.

## Limits worth knowing

- **Chrome only.** It doesn't appear in Safari, Firefox or Edge.
- **Internet required.** Audio is processed by Google.
- **Only inside Docs** (and Slides speaker notes). It won't help you in Slack or email.
- **Stops if you click away.** Switching windows usually stops listening.

## Alternatives on a Mac

**Apple Dictation** works in Google Docs in any browser: click into the document and press your Dictation shortcut. See [how to use dictation on Mac](/blog/how-to-use-dictation-on-mac).

**A dictation app** gives you the same tool everywhere: Docs, Slack, email. With [MacVoice](/) you click into the document, press Control + Option + Space and talk. Text appears at the cursor, fillers are removed, and "bullet point" starts a list item. Because MacVoice types as keyboard input, Google Docs treats it exactly like typing, including in Safari and Arc.

| | Google Docs voice typing | Apple Dictation | MacVoice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Browsers | Chrome only | Any | Any |
| Works outside Docs | No | Yes | Yes |
| Internet needed | Yes | Not for on-device languages | No (after one-time model download) |
| Removes "um" | No | No | Yes |
| Editing commands | Yes (English) | Basic | "scratch that" |
| Price | Free | Free | Free |

## Tips for writing long documents by voice

- Dictate a **rough first draft fast**, then edit with the keyboard. Mixing the two is slower. Our guide to [writing a first draft by voice](/blog/write-first-draft-by-voice) has a method.
- Use **headings as signposts**: type the headings first, then dictate under each one.
