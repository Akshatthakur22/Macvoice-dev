Speech recognition can only transcribe what the microphone hears. Here's how to pick and set up a mic for voice typing, without overspending.

## The one rule: closer is better

A microphone close to your mouth hears **your voice loud and the room quiet**. That ratio matters more than the mic's price or specs.

## Options compared

| Microphone | Quiet room | Noisy / shared room | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| MacBook built-in | Good | Weak | Recent MacBooks have good mic arrays; they still hear the whole room |
| iMac / Studio Display built-in | Good | Weak | Same as above |
| AirPods / earbuds | Good | Good | Close-ish to the mouth; convenient |
| Headset with boom mic | Very good | Very good | Best accuracy per dollar for all-day use |
| USB desk mic | Very good if close | Okay | Must be near your mouth, ideally on an arm |
| Webcam mic | Fair | Poor | Usually far away; a common accidental default |

## Select the right input

Macs often switch inputs when you connect AirPods or a webcam.

- **System Settings → Sound → Input:** pick your mic and watch the level bars move as you speak.
- **Apple Dictation:** System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation → *Microphone source*. Set a specific mic instead of *Automatic* if it keeps choosing the wrong one.
- **Dictation apps** generally use the system's default input. [MacVoice](/) uses the current system input.

## Set the level

Aim for bars that move clearly when you speak normally but don't max out. Too quiet, and words are lost; too loud, and audio distorts.

## Quick wins without buying anything

- Sit **facing** your Mac, within arm's length.
- **Close the window** and turn off fans.
- Use **AirPods** if you have them, in shared spaces.

## When to invest

If you dictate for hours a day, a comfortable **headset with a boom mic** is the best upgrade: accurate, consistent and kind to your voice because you don't have to project ([RSI and voice care](/blog/voice-typing-for-rsi-and-wrist-pain)).
