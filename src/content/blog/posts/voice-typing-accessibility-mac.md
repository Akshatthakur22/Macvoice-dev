Voice input isn't just a productivity trick. For many people it's how they use a computer at all. macOS has some of the best built-in voice tools of any platform.

## The built-in options

### Dictation: type by voice

Press a key, speak, text appears. Best for writing. [Full guide](/blog/how-to-use-dictation-on-mac).

### Voice Control: run the Mac by voice

**System Settings → Accessibility → Voice Control.** Once on, you can:

- Open and switch apps ("open Mail", "switch to Safari").
- Click anything: say **"show numbers"** and then a number, or **"show grid"** to click anywhere.
- Scroll, drag and use menus.
- Dictate and edit: "select previous sentence", "delete that".
- Add custom words in **Vocabulary**, and create your own commands.

Note: when Voice Control is on it replaces standard Dictation. [Voice Control vs Dictation](/blog/voice-control-vs-dictation-mac).

### Spoken Content: hear it back

**System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content.** Turn on *Speak selection*, select text and press the shortcut (Option-Esc by default) to hear it read aloud. Useful for checking dictated text without reading it, and helpful for people with dyslexia or low vision.

## Who benefits most

- **Motor impairments, RSI, injuries:** Voice Control for navigation, dictation for text ([RSI setup](/blog/voice-typing-for-rsi-and-wrist-pain)).
- **Dyslexia and spelling difficulties:** dictation removes the spelling barrier; Spoken Content helps proof-read.
- **Low vision:** dictation plus VoiceOver or Spoken Content.
- **Temporary situations:** a broken wrist, a baby on one arm.

## Where third-party dictation apps fit

Built-in tools are free and capable. Dictation apps add things some people find important:

- **Automatic cleanup** of "um" and stutters, so less editing (which may be the hardest part).
- **"Scratch that"** to undo by voice.
- **Custom words** without switching Voice Control on.
- **Chat-safe line breaks** so a "new line" doesn't send a message.
- **The same behaviour in every app.**

[MacVoice](/) does these, on-device. It doesn't replace Voice Control's navigation; many people use both: Voice Control for clicking and moving around, MacVoice for writing.

## Getting started tips

- Start with **one task** (emails, say) for a week.
- Learn the **five commands** you'll use most: new line, new paragraph, comma, question mark, scratch that ([cheat sheet](/blog/mac-voice-typing-commands)).
- Expect a learning curve of a few days. It gets much easier.
