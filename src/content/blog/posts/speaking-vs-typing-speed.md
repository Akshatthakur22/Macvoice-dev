"Voice typing is 3× faster" is a popular claim. It's roughly true, with a few important footnotes.

![Speaking vs typing speed](/blog/diagrams/speaking-vs-typing-speed.svg "English text entry speed in the Stanford/UW/Baidu study.")

## What the research says

### Speech vs phone keyboard

In 2016, researchers from **Stanford, the University of Washington and Baidu** (Ruan, Wobbrock, Liou, Ng and Landay) had 32 people enter text on an iPhone using either the keyboard or Baidu's Deep Speech 2 recognition. In the peer-reviewed 2017 version:

- **English:** speech was about **2.9× faster**: **153 vs 52 words per minute**.
- **Mandarin:** about **2.8× faster**.
- Error rates *during* entry were lower with speech; after correction, speech left slightly more errors in English.

The tests were done in a quiet lab, a best case.

### Desktop typing

A 2018 study of **136 million keystrokes** from 168,000 volunteers (Dhakal, Feit, Kristensson and Oulasvirta) found an average desktop typing speed of about **52 words per minute**, with fast typists well above 80 and many people below 40.

## The real-world gain

Raw speaking speed isn't the whole story:

- **Thinking time** is the same either way. You can't talk faster than you can think of what to say.
- **Corrections** take time, especially names and jargon ([fix those](/blog/why-dictation-gets-names-wrong)).
- **Editing style:** spoken drafts are wordier, so you'll trim more.

In practice, many people find dictation **noticeably faster for long-form prose** (emails, documents, messages, AI prompts) and *not* faster for short edits, code or forms.

## Estimate your own savings

1. Time yourself typing a typical 200-word email.
2. Dictate a similar email, including fixing errors.
3. Multiply the difference by how many such emails you write per week.

If you type 40 wpm and write 3,000 words of prose a day, that's about 75 minutes of typing. Even a 2× real-world gain saves over half an hour a day.

## Making the gain bigger

- **Separate talking and editing** ([method](/blog/write-first-draft-by-voice)).
- **Use cleanup** so you're not deleting "um"s ([filler removal](/blog/why-dictation-removes-filler-words)).
- **Add custom words** for names and jargon.
- **Use a tool that types as you speak** so you catch problems immediately, like [MacVoice](/).
