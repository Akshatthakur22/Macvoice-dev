Most people try dictation for long writing once, stop every three sentences to fix a word, and conclude it's slower than typing. It's not dictation that's slow; it's **editing while dictating**.

## The method: talk, then edit

### 1. Outline with the keyboard (5 minutes)

Type the headings or the 5–8 points you want to make. This is your map; it stops you rambling.

### 2. Dictate each section like you're explaining it

Click under the first heading and talk. Imagine a friend asked "so what's this part about?" and answer them. Don't aim for perfect sentences.

### 3. Don't fix anything while you're talking

Misheard word? Clumsy sentence? Ignore it. Keep going to the end of the section. Every time you stop to fix, you lose your train of thought.

### 4. Edit with the keyboard

Now go back and edit: fix words, cut repetition, tighten sentences. Spoken drafts are wordier than typed ones, so expect to cut 10–20%.

## Why it's faster

People speak far faster than they type. In a Stanford-led study, English speech input was about **2.9× faster** than a phone keyboard (153 vs 52 words per minute). At a desk the gap is smaller, but still large ([details](/blog/speaking-vs-typing-speed)). Even after editing, you usually come out ahead, and the draft is often more natural.

## Make the draft cleaner as you speak

- **Speak in full sentences** and pause *between* them, not inside them, so punctuation lands in the right place.
- **Use "new paragraph"** when you change idea.
- **Use a tool that removes "um"s.** Apple Dictation types every filler; [MacVoice](/)'s Polished mode removes them and fixes small things like "dont" → "don't". Less to clean up later ([why filler removal matters](/blog/why-dictation-removes-filler-words)).
- **Say "scratch that"** (MacVoice) to delete a sentence you didn't like, instead of reaching for the keyboard.

## Practical setup

- A **headset or AirPods** if you're not in a quiet room ([microphone guide](/blog/best-microphone-for-dictation)).
- **Water nearby.** Dictating for an hour is surprisingly tiring for your voice; take breaks.
- **Short sessions:** 20–30 minutes per section is plenty.

## Where it fits best

First drafts of blog posts, essays, reports, newsletters, book chapters, speeches. Anywhere the hard part is getting words out, not polishing them. For building the habit, see [how to get better at dictation](/blog/how-to-get-better-at-dictation).
