These words get used interchangeably, but they describe different jobs, and picking the wrong kind of tool is a common frustration.

## The terms

| Term | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Speech-to-text (STT) / speech recognition (ASR)** | The technology that turns spoken audio into text | Apple SpeechAnalyzer, OpenAI Whisper |
| **Dictation / voice typing** | Speaking *to write*, live, into the app you're using | Writing an email by talking |
| **Transcription** | Turning *recorded* audio into a text document | Meeting notes, interview transcripts, subtitles |
| **Voice recognition / speaker recognition** | Identifying *who* is speaking | Voice ID, "Hey Siri" recognising your voice |
| **Voice control / voice commands** | Controlling a device by speaking | "Open Safari", "click Done" |

## Dictation: you're the writer

In dictation you **compose** text by speaking. You want:

- text to appear where your cursor is, in any app,
- punctuation and formatting commands ("new paragraph"),
- cleanup (no "um"s),
- low delay, so you can see what you're writing.

Tools: Apple Dictation, Windows voice typing, Google Docs voice typing, dictation apps like [MacVoice](/).

## Transcription: you're capturing what was said

In transcription, the audio already exists, or it's a conversation between several people. You want:

- support for **audio and video files**,
- **speaker labels** (who said what),
- **timestamps**,
- export to documents or subtitles.

Tools: meeting recorders, transcription services, file transcription apps like MacWhisper.

## Why the difference matters

- A **dictation app won't transcribe your Zoom recording.** It listens to your microphone, live.
- A **meeting transcriber is clumsy for writing an email.** It produces a transcript, not text at your cursor.
- **Cleanup differs:** dictation should remove your "um"s; a legal or research transcript may need to keep them.
