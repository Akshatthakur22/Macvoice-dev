Speech recognition used to work best for one kind of voice. It's much better now, but your settings still make a big difference.

## 1. Choose your regional language

Most dictation engines offer regional English variants such as **US, UK, Australia, Canada, India, Ireland, Singapore, South Africa** (exact lists vary by engine). Each is trained on speech from that region, including local names, place names and pronunciation.

- **Mac Dictation:** System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation → Languages → Edit ([multi-language guide](/blog/dictate-in-multiple-languages-mac)).
- **[MacVoice](/):** follows your Mac's language and region (System Settings → General → Language & Region). If Apple's model for exactly that region (say English (India)) is installed, it uses it; otherwise it uses an installed model of the same language, and only downloads one if none is installed.

## 2. Teach it local names

Names are where accent-related errors pile up: people, places, food, companies. Add them as [custom words](/blog/add-custom-words-mac-dictation). A list of 20–30 often fixes most errors.

## 3. Speak naturally

Don't try to sound American or British. Imitating an accent makes your speech less consistent, which hurts accuracy. Speak at your normal pace, clearly, in full phrases.

## 4. Mixing languages

Hinglish, Spanglish and other code-switching are still hard for most engines. Choose the language most of the sentence is in, and add frequently borrowed words as custom words.

## 5. Microphone and room

Accent plus background noise is harder than either alone. A close microphone helps more than anything else ([microphone guide](/blog/best-microphone-for-dictation)).

## 6. Measure, don't guess

Read the same 100-word paragraph with two regional settings and count errors. Keep the better one. That's your personal [word error rate](/blog/word-error-rate-explained).

## Which engines handle accents well?

Large modern models trained on diverse speech (Apple's on-device engine, Whisper, major cloud services) are all far better than older systems. Differences between them depend heavily on your specific accent and vocabulary, so a quick personal test beats any general ranking. See [Whisper vs Apple speech recognition](/blog/whisper-vs-apple-speech-recognition).
