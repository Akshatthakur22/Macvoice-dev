macOS has two different ways to use your voice, and they're easy to confuse. The short version: **Dictation is a voice keyboard. Voice Control is a voice mouse *and* keyboard.**

## Side by side

| | Dictation | Voice Control |
|---|---|---|
| Where to turn it on | System Settings → Keyboard | System Settings → Accessibility → Voice Control |
| What it's for | Typing text | Controlling the whole Mac: click, scroll, open apps, edit, type |
| How you use it | Press a shortcut, talk, stop | Always listening while on |
| Editing by voice | Basic (punctuation, capitals) | Rich: "select previous word", "delete that", "correct [word]" |
| Custom vocabulary | No | Yes (Vocabulary list) |
| Best for | Everyday writing | Hands-free computer use, motor disabilities, RSI |

## Dictation: talk to type

Dictation is the one most people want. Press the Microphone key or your shortcut, speak, and the words appear. It handles punctuation commands like "comma" and "new paragraph" and stops when you press Escape. Full guide: [how to use dictation on Mac](/blog/how-to-use-dictation-on-mac).

## Voice Control: run your Mac by voice

Voice Control is an accessibility feature introduced in macOS Catalina. When it's on, a microphone window stays on screen and your Mac listens for commands:

- **"Open Safari"**, **"scroll down"**, **"click Done"**.
- **"Show numbers"** puts a number next to everything clickable; say the number to click it. **"Hide numbers"** removes them.
- **"Show grid"** lets you click anywhere by narrowing down a numbered grid.
- In text fields you can dictate, then edit: "select previous sentence", "delete that", "capitalize that".

It also has a **Vocabulary** list (in Voice Control settings) where you can add names and terms it should recognise. Normal Dictation doesn't have that. See [how to add custom words](/blog/add-custom-words-mac-dictation).

## The catch: you can't use both at once

When Voice Control is turned on, **it replaces standard Dictation**. If your Dictation shortcut suddenly stopped working, check whether Voice Control got switched on.

Voice Control is also always listening, which can be a problem in meetings or open offices. You can say "go to sleep" to pause it and "wake up" to resume.

## Which should you use?

- **You just want to write faster:** Dictation, or a dictation app.
- **You need to use your Mac without a keyboard or mouse** (injury, disability, RSI flare-up): Voice Control. It's one of the best accessibility tools on any platform. See our [Mac voice accessibility guide](/blog/voice-typing-accessibility-mac).
- **You want both:** use Voice Control for navigation and switch it off when you're writing at length.

## Where MacVoice fits

[MacVoice](/) is a dictation tool, not a Voice Control replacement. It can't click buttons. It sits alongside normal Dictation's role, with extra cleanup ("um" removal), "scratch that", custom words and chat-safe line breaks. If you rely on Voice Control for navigation, note that turning it on replaces Apple Dictation but doesn't affect MacVoice, which has its own shortcut.
